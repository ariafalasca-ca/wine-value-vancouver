Finding 1 · What a point costs

Each extra point comes with a price about 13% higher

Median bottle price at each scoreAdjusted trend: +12.8% per point (slope only)90 points ("Excellent")

Price and score rise together, but not in lockstep. Some groups get their points far more cheaply than others, and that gap is what a shopper can use.

After controlling for critic, grape and country, each point goes with a 12.8% higher price (95% interval 12.7% to 12.9%). The step from 89 to 90 points raises the median price by 15%, in line with the three steps on either side of it (8% to 27%).

Darker cells hold more reviews; hover a cell or the gold dot for its numbers. Typical score: least-squares fit on ln(price) and its square, 111,538 reviews. The ranking also adjusts for vintage and, in one of two ways, for the critic (see backup).

For questions · backup

Four questions we expect, and our answers

"Do critics taste what I taste?"

No, and we don't claim they do.

In 6,175 blind ratings of 523 wines, a 10-times pricier wine scored about 7 points higher with experts but 4 lower with regular drinkers (Goldstein et al., 2008). We rank by blind expert score: for the same price, you get more points.

"Why use US data for a Vancouver shopper?"

US data ranks the groups; BC data checks the shelf.

US$ prices only place a wine among its price peers. Adding the vintage year leaves 10 of the red and 9 of the white $16–25 top 10 in place (rank correlation 0.98 and 0.99). The 2026 BC list shows if a pick is sold here and what it costs. The score ranking keeps historical US price benchmarks separate from the BC shelf check. BC shelves changed too: US wines were pulled from BC government stores in March 2025.

"Doesn't one critic skew a whole country?"

It could, so we score every group both ways.

With and without a critic adjustment, and we keep the more cautious result. Austria shows why: +1.6 without the adjustment, only +0.3 with it. Every pick names its critic.

"Don't cheap wines look too good?"

A little, so we lead with US$16–25.

Wine Enthusiast never publishes wines below 80 points. 6.7% of wines up to $15 score 80 to 82, against 2.4% at $16 to 25, so the cheapest tier is missing its worst wines.

Methods and robustness checks

Points above price

With critic adj.: points ~ ln(price) + ln(price)² + critic + vintage group Without: points ~ ln(price) + ln(price)² + vintage group gap = actual points − fitted points

Groups: country plus grape within a US$ tier, at least 30 reviews. Range: mean ± 1.96 × SD ÷ √n. Cautious score: best rows use the lower of the two low ends, weakest rows the higher of the two high ends. R² 0.44 with the critic adjustment, 0.41 without. Each shelf section ranked on its own; 2.8% of wines fit none.

Data

Wine Enthusiast via Kaggle: 129,971 reviews, 111,538 with price and country (prices are missing more often for France, 20%, and Austria, 16%, than for the US, 0.4%, so the sample leans toward wines with a listed import price); prices are the producer's suggested retail in US$ at review.
