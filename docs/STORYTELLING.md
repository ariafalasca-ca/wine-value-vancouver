# From a consumer question to a data story

## A familiar decision

The pitch begins with choosing wine under a budget. A critic score is recognizable, but the same score can appear at very different prices. That tension gives the audience a reason to care before introducing the model.

## A shortcut worth questioning

The story tests an intuitive metric: the share of wines scoring 90 or more. Showing that it largely follows price motivates a different benchmark. The analytical method enters as the answer to a practical problem rather than as an isolated technical exercise.

## A metric the audience can explain

“Points above price” turns a residual into a comparison: how many critic points does a wine earn beyond what its historical price predicts? The two-model critic check introduces uncertainty without requiring the audience to choose one treatment of critic effects.

## An interactive choice

The shelf guide lets the audience change wine type and US price tier. Rankings become something to explore, while the BC price check brings the recommendation back to the shopper's local budget. Group estimates, snapshot prices and critic assessments remain distinct from individual taste and current availability.

## A memorable close

The pitch narrows the conclusion to two categories to remember: Portuguese red and Australian Shiraz. The recommendation is specific enough to communicate, with its historical scope retained.
